A grade says how a token is built; this page says what happens when one of its parts fails. Every failure mode in the catalogue — your keys stolen, the custodian bankrupt, the company acquired, the multiplier set wrong, a regulator at the door — are put to every issuer in the same words, so an answer one issuer can give and another cannot is visible as exactly that. An outcome is never invented. It is documented only when the issuer's or a regulator's own document addresses the case and we can show the words; inferred when the documents do not address it but the structure implies the answer, and we say that is our reading; litigated when a court, tribunal or regulator decided this or a materially identical case, with the case cited; unknown when we looked and the documents do not say — and then where we looked is on the record, because the gap is itself the finding. A cell with nothing in it means nobody has answered that question for that issuer yet, and it is drawn rather than left blank.

    The matrix

    One row per question, one column per issuer. Tap a cell to read the answer itself — the outcome, the quote it rests on, the source with its locator and the date it was read. The status filter keeps the questions in which at least one issuer's answer has one of the statuses you picked and marks those cells; it cannot hide individual cells, because a matrix with holes in it is not a matrix. The actor filter keeps the questions about that part of the chain. Both live in the URL, so a filtered view is a link you can send.
